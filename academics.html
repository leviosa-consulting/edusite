Academics

Rigor with warmth, from kindergarten to commencement

A deliberately sequenced K–12 curriculum taught in small, discussion-driven classes.

Divisions

Three schools, one path

Grades K–5

Lower School

Phonics-based literacy, Singapore-style mathematics, and science taught through experiment and observation. Every student studies music, visual art, and Spanish from kindergarten, and spends time each week in our outdoor classroom.

Grades 6–8

Middle School

Departmentalized instruction begins, along with laboratory science, pre-algebra through geometry, and a two-year world history sequence. Electives open up — robotics, drama, journalism, orchestra — and every student joins an advisory of eight.

Grades 9–12

Upper School

Eighteen Advanced Placement courses, honors tracks in every discipline, and a senior capstone: a year-long independent research project defended before a faculty panel. College counseling begins in grade ten.

Departments

Signature programs

Areas where Ashford's program goes well beyond the standard curriculum.

∑

Mathematics & Sciences

Lab-first science from grade six and a math program that lets students accelerate through multivariable calculus.

✎

Humanities

A seminar-style English and history sequence built around primary sources, debate, and serious writing.

♪

Arts

Orchestra, choir, theatre, and studio art with dedicated faculty and two public performances each year.

⌘

Technology & Innovation

Computer science from grade three, competitive robotics, and an engineering design studio open after hours.

⚘

World Languages

Spanish from kindergarten; French, Latin, and Mandarin from grade six, all taught for real fluency.

⚑

Athletics

Fourteen interscholastic teams and a no-cut policy through middle school — everyone plays.

Beyond the Classroom

Learning that leaves the building

  • Grade-level expeditions. From tide pools in grade two to a capital-city civics week in grade eight.
  • Service learning. Every division partners with local organizations for sustained, hands-on service.
  • Global exchange. Upper School students may spend a term with partner schools abroad.
  • Visiting scholars. Authors, scientists, and artists spend residencies on campus each year.
